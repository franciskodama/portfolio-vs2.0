What I saw on housesigma.com · Nov 2026

A research tool that is also a brokerage.

HouseSigma is a listings portal, a data company and a licensed brokerage in one. That combination is the opportunity: the data earns trust, and the brokerage earns revenue when a user chooses a HouseSigma agent to buy or sell.

Rich listing pages
Listing and sold history, SigmaEstimate, rental yield, cash flow, comparables, a Popularity score, a market demand meter, school scores and community stats. Every ingredient of an offer decision is already on the page.
Ingredients, no recipe
Those modules sit in separate tabs. The buyer has to combine sold comparables, days on market and popularity in their head to arrive at one number: the price to offer.
Gaps on new listings
On a listing added the day I visited, SigmaEstimate showed "Insufficient data". New listings are exactly where buyers move fastest and need an anchor most.
Built for first-time viewers
"Schedule Viewing" is the right step on a first visit. Returning buyers who have already toured are ready for a different one.
A shifting market
HouseSigma's own August infographic reported that four in five GTA homes sold below asking. When the answer to "over or under asking?" changes listing by listing, guidance is worth more.

01 · The problem and the opportunity

The audience is huge at research. The revenue is at the offer.

Business outcome: grow brokerage revenue by increasing the share of HouseSigma users who transact with a HouseSigma agent.

The leak (hypothesis to validate): users do weeks of research on HouseSigma, then make the most valuable decision of the journey, the offer, somewhere else and with someone else. The platform carries the cost of acquiring them and a competitor's agent collects the commission.

The opportunity: be the most useful voice at the moment of decision. If HouseSigma is where buyers decide what to offer, it is the natural place to choose who submits it.

The job to be done

When I find a home I want, I want to know what price will actually win it, so I don't overpay or lose another bidding war.

This is a high-stakes, high-anxiety, high-frequency job: a typical buyer repeats it on several homes before one offer lands. Each repetition is a chance to earn trust.

Opportunity solution tree (Teresa Torres)

The other two opportunities are real. Offer Insight addresses them as a side effect: it shows the agent adding value before asking for anything, and it brings the agent in at the moment of need.

02 · The solution

Offer Insight. One panel, one clear answer.

A new module at the top of every for-sale listing that combines the data HouseSigma already has into a single, explainable recommendation. Full detail for signed-in users, a teaser for everyone else, matching how sold data is already gated today.

Two layers, two moments. While buyers are still evaluating, every viewer sees one line under the asking price: "Likely sells for $930K to $945K". Once a buyer shows intent (a return visit, Watch, or "I've toured this home"), the main button changes from "Schedule Viewing" to "Plan my offer" and opens the full panel in the Estimates section.

Answer

Likely sold range

"Similar homes here sold for 94% to 96% of asking." On a hot listing, it could be 103% to 105%. Built from sold comparables, adjusted for days on market, Popularity and market demand. Always a range, never a fake-precise number.

Explore

Offer simulator

The buyer moves one slider and sees where their offer lands against the range, how competitive it is, and the monthly payment it implies. It turns anxiety into a plan.

Trust

Show the work

The comparables behind the range, their sold-to-list ratios and the signals used, one tap away. Transparency is HouseSigma's brand: it was first to publish sold prices.

Convert

Warm agent hand-off

"Review this offer with a HouseSigma agent." The agent receives the listing, the buyer's offer and the analysis, so the first call starts at the decision, not at "hi, how can I help?"

      04 · Why it's a game changer · Marty Cagan's four risks

      Valuable, usable, feasible, viable.

      A good bet answers all four before engineering starts. Here is my outside-in view, and the cheapest test that would prove me wrong on each.

      Valuable · will customers want it?

      It answers the question they already ask.

      "What should I offer?" is the highest-stakes question in the buyer journey and it repeats on every home they consider. It extends HouseSigma's promise, "make better real estate decisions", from research into action.

      Cheapest testPainted door: an "Offer Insight" entry on 5% of listing pages, plus eight interviews with buyers who closed in the last six months.

      Usable · can they figure it out?

      One range, one slider, one sentence.

      Progressive disclosure: the answer first, the evidence one tap away. It reuses patterns users already know on HouseSigma (Popularity, the market demand meter, the mortgage calculator), so there is nothing new to learn.

      Cheapest testFive moderated sessions on this prototype. Pass line: four of five buyers pick an opening offer and explain why, in under 60 seconds.

      Feasible · can we build it?

      The data is already there.

      Version 1 needs no new model: the typical range of sold-to-asking ratios for similar homes in the same community, adjusted by days on market and Popularity. The SigmaEstimate team can upgrade it later. Compliance with TRESA, RECO advertising rules and board data licences (sold data stays behind sign-in) needs early review with legal.

      Cheapest testBacktest on 12 months of solds. Pass line: at least 70% of homes sell inside the predicted range.

      Viable · does it work for the business?

      It monetizes trust HouseSigma already earned.

      Every hand-off is a lead with intent, a specific home and a price. That is worth far more than a generic "contact an agent" form. It also feeds registrations, because the full range requires sign-in.

      Cheapest testWith the real funnel numbers, work out how many extra deals a month pay for the team. Then check agent capacity: can the team answer a hand-off within one hour?

      How we'll know it worked

      Outcomes over outputs.

      Primary metricAgent reviews requested per 1,000 listing views
      • Measured in an A/B test, signed-in users, GTA first
      • Decision rule, written before launch: ship if the lift is 20% or more and the confidence interval excludes zero
      Input metricsThe path to the outcome
      • Offer Insight opened, per listing view
      • Simulator used (offer changed at least once)
      • Registrations started from the teaser
      • Review to offer submitted
      GuardrailsWhat must not get worse
      • Range accuracy: 70% or more sell inside it
      • Agent first response under 1 hour
      • Watchlist and saved-search rates

      05 · The plan

      Learn first. Then build. Then scale.

      Now

      Discover

      • Eight buyer interviews, one past offer each
      • Painted door on 5% of listings
      • Backtest range accuracy on 12 months of solds
      • Legal review: TRESA, RECO, board data rules

      Gate: click-through and accuracy pass lines met.

      Next

      Deliver v1

      • Rules-based range for one market: GTA townhouses and condos
      • Simulator plus hand-off routed to agents
      • 50/50 A/B test against today's listing page

      Gate: primary metric lift with guardrails held.

      Later

      Scale

      • All Ontario markets, then BC and Alberta
      • Model upgrade with the SigmaEstimate team
      • App alerts: "a price drop puts your offer in range"
      • Pricing Insight for sellers

      Gate: deals attributed per agent stay healthy.
